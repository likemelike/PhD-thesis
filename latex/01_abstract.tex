%随着 GPS 技术的快速发展和智能设备的普及，位置点、轨迹与路径等时空数据在规模上呈指数级增长。
海量、多源、高频且动态演化的时空数据，为城市计算中的高效算法设计提供了广阔而富有挑战性的应用场景，催生了许多基于地理位置的服务。城市计算是利用大规模时空数据，结合数据挖掘、人工智能和高效算法模型，对城市运行进行分析和优化的一门交叉研究领域。
本文分别针对城市计算中路径规划、轨迹模式挖掘、时空密切接触发现以及轨迹相似度连接等流行的应用需求，通过系统调研已有研究工作，提出并研究了以下四个问题并取得了创新性成果。

(1) 提出并研究了城市计算中面向大规模行程请求流的持续最优路线组合规划。在路径规划服务使用越来越频繁的情况下，在通勤时间等高峰期大量用户会在极短的时间间隔内密集地发布行程请求，从而形成了持续的行程请求流。本问题研究目标是在一个动态路网上为一组行程请求流实时规划一个路线组合使得所有路线的总通行时间最小。该问题本质上需要在指数级规模的路径组合空间中进行搜索。随着行程请求数量的增加，可行的路线组合候选数目呈指数级增长，其精确求解的时间复杂度难以控制在可接受范围内，因而无法满足实际应用中的实时性需求。为高效求解该问题，设计了一种自感知批处理算法。该算法包含两个主要技术步骤：初始路径搜索和批量优化处理。对于一批新到达的行程请求流，先执行初始路径搜索为其生成一组可避免部分交通拥堵的初始路径组合。 为了进一步避免潜在的交通拥堵，对最近发布的请求集合进执行批量优化处理，对初始路径组合进行进一步优化。在真实数据集上的实验结果表明，提出的方法优于基线方法，能够降低超过 $40\%$ 的总通行时间。 
(2)  提出并研究了城市计算中一种放宽的伴随群体联合移动模式挖掘。现有群体模式定义及其对应方法在同时兼顾模式表达能力与计算效率方面仍存在明显不足，难以在大规模数据环境中实现高效、稳定的持续挖掘。一个放宽的伴随群体由多个移动对象组成，这些对象需要在多个时间段内在空间上保持接近，并允许对象在时间维度上暂时离开群体而不破坏整体模式的有效性。在现实应用中，，联合移动模式挖掘任务往往需要以连续或增量的方式执行。这不仅要求算法在时间复杂度上具备良好的可扩展性，还要求其在模式定义上具有一定的灵活性，以适应动态变化的移动行为。为解决该问题，首先设计了一种精确搜索算法。该算法对候选对象对及候选群体进行精确验证，能够保证结果的完备性。为进一步满足实时应用需求，额外提出了一种社区感知的近似搜索算法。通过近似计算与结构化聚类方法显著压缩搜索空间，从而高效解决伴随群体发现问题。基于真实数据集的实验结果表明，所提出的社区感知算法能够从大规模轨迹数据中高效发现伴随群体，验证了方法在实际应用场景中的有效性。

(3) 提出并研究了城市计算中面向大规模移动对象的持续暴露接触发现问题。已有方法大多假设数据完整可得，缺乏对持续到达的轨迹位置流的支持。给定一组移动对象，以及查询集合用于表示当前已确认的暴露接触案例，目标是在轨迹数据持续到达和更新的环境下，动态维护并实时更新该查询集合，从而持续检测新的暴露接触案例。为此，设计了一个精确分组处理算法和一个近似分组处理算法，以及对应的分组过滤与预检查等优化策略。实验结果表明与精确方法相比，所提出的分组处理框架在不同数据集和参数设置下均能够有效减少候选对象数量并显著降低时间开销。

(4) 提出并研究了城市计算中运动范围感知的轨迹相似度连接问题。由于采样频率不一致、定位误差不可避免、以及相邻采样点之间运动过程不可观测等因素，轨迹数据往往具有不确定性与稀疏性特征。现有多数相似度连接方法主要基于离散采样点进行距离计算，难以有效反映对象在采样间隔内的连续运动状态，从而可能遗漏在现实中具有重要意义的潜在交互行为。不同于传统基于点邻近关系的建模方式，本研究以对象在给定时间区间内的潜在运动范围为基本分析单元，引入交集相似度度量，用于量化两个对象运动范围之间的重叠程度。针对 交集相似度连接 在连续时间区间下计算复杂、候选对象对规模庞大的挑战，本文提出了一种由时间区间运动范围引导的高效求解框架。具体而言，设计了混合球树索引结构，用于层次化组织对象在时间区间内的运动范围表示，并结合重划分策略与多级剪枝技术，实现对候选对象对的有效过滤。

综上，本文对城市计算中路径规划、轨迹模式挖掘、时空密切接触发现以及轨迹相似度连接进行了研究，提出了高效的解决方案，可广泛适用于城市交通拥堵预防、拼车推荐、疾病传播控制、城市交通模式分析以及异常轨迹检测等场景。

\enabstract
Massive, multi-source, high-frequency, and dynamically evolving spatio-temporal data provide broad and challenging application scenarios for efficient algorithm design in urban computing. These data have also led to many location-based services. Urban computing is an interdisciplinary research field. It uses large-scale spatio-temporal data and combines data mining, artificial intelligence, and efficient algorithm models to analyze and optimize urban operations. This dissertation focuses on several popular application needs in urban computing, including route planning, trajectory pattern mining, spatio-temporal close contact discovery, and trajectory similarity join. Through a systematic survey of existing studies, this dissertation proposes and investigates the following four problems and achieves several innovative results.

(1) This dissertation proposes and studies continuous optimal route combination planning for large-scale trip request streams in urban computing. As route planning services are used more frequently, a large number of users may submit trip requests within very short time intervals, especially during peak periods such as commuting hours. This forms a continuous stream of trip requests. The goal of this problem is to plan, in real time, a combination of routes for a group of trip request streams on a dynamic road network so that the total travel time of all routes is minimized. The problem essentially requires searching in an exponentially large space of route combinations. As the number of trip requests increases, the number of feasible route combinations grows exponentially. Therefore, the time complexity of exact solutions becomes difficult to control within an acceptable range and cannot meet the real-time requirements in practice. To solve this problem efficiently, a self-aware batch processing algorithm is designed. The algorithm contains two main steps: initial route search and batch optimization. For a batch of newly arrived trip request streams, the algorithm first performs initial route search to generate a set of initial route combinations that can avoid some traffic congestion. To further reduce potential congestion, a batch optimization process is applied to the most recently released requests to further improve the initial route combinations.Experimental results on real datasets show that the proposed method outperforms the baseline methods and can reduce the total travel time by more than $40\%$.

(2) This dissertation proposes and studies a relaxed accompanying group co-movement pattern mining problem in urban computing. Existing group pattern definitions and their methods still have clear limitations in balancing pattern expressiveness and computational efficiency. As a result, it is difficult to perform efficient and stable mining on large-scale data. A relaxed accompanying group consists of multiple moving objects. These objects need to stay spatially close during several time periods, while temporary departures of some objects are allowed in the time dimension without breaking the validity of the overall pattern. In real applications, co-movement pattern mining tasks are often performed in a continuous or incremental manner. This requires the algorithm to have good scalability in time complexity and sufficient flexibility in pattern definition to adapt to dynamically changing mobility behaviors. To address this problem, an exact search algorithm is first designed. This algorithm performs precise verification on candidate object pairs and candidate groups, and guarantees the completeness of the results. To further meet real-time application requirements, a community-aware approximate search algorithm is also proposed. It significantly reduces the search space through approximate computation and structured clustering, and efficiently solves the accompanying group discovery problem. Experimental results on real-world datasets show that the proposed community-aware algorithm can efficiently discover accompanying groups from large-scale trajectory data, which demonstrates its effectiveness in practical applications.

(3) This dissertation proposes and studies the continuous exposure contact discovery problem for large-scale moving objects in urban computing. Most existing methods assume that data are complete and available, and they lack support for continuously arriving trajectory location streams.
Given a set of moving objects and a query set that represents currently confirmed exposure contact cases, the goal is to dynamically maintain and update this query set in an environment where trajectory data continuously arrive and are updated. In this way, new exposure contact cases can be continuously detected in real time.
To address this problem, an exact group processing algorithm and an approximate group processing algorithm are designed, together with optimization strategies such as group filtering and pre-checking. Experimental results show that, compared with the exact method, the proposed group processing framework can effectively reduce the number of candidate objects and significantly lower the time cost under different datasets and parameter settings.

(4) This dissertation proposes and studies the movement-range-aware trajectory similarity join problem in urban computing. Due to inconsistent sampling frequencies, unavoidable positioning errors, and the unobservable movement between adjacent sampling points, trajectory data often show uncertainty and sparsity. Most existing similarity join methods compute distances based on discrete sampled points. Therefore, they cannot effectively reflect the continuous movement of objects within sampling intervals, and may miss potential interaction behaviors that are important in reality.
Different from traditional methods based on point proximity, this study uses the potential movement range of an object within a given time interval as the basic analysis unit. An intersection similarity measure is introduced to quantify the overlap between the movement ranges of two objects.
The intersection similarity join over continuous time intervals is computationally complex and may generate a large number of candidate object pairs. To address this challenge, this dissertation proposes an efficient solution framework guided by movement ranges within time intervals. Specifically, a hybrid ball-tree index structure is designed to hierarchically organize the movement range representations of objects within time intervals. It is combined with a repartition strategy and multi-level pruning techniques to effectively filter candidate object pairs.

In summary, this dissertation studies route planning, trajectory pattern mining, spatio-temporal close contact discovery, and trajectory similarity join in urban computing. Efficient solutions are proposed for these problems. The proposed methods can be widely applied in scenarios such as urban traffic congestion prevention, ride-sharing recommendation, disease spread control, urban traffic pattern analysis, and abnormal trajectory detection.
